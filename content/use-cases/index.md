# What informs our decision, what changed since we last looked, and why did we decide that

</div>
<div class="prose">
Aster Biologics is piloting an electronic batch-record system on one production line. Before it goes live, a consultant and the client's compliance lead must say which requirements of 21 CFR Part 11 apply to this workflow, and whether the client's own procedures meet them. Five requirements are in scope. Aster, its people and its documents are fictional. The regulation and the FDA guidance are real.

</div>

## 1. What informs our decision?

**Bring text, numbers, calculations and relationships together into one managed basis for judgment.**

An assessment references a combination of inputs. For Aster's the combination is the clause in 21 CFR 11.10(k), the consultant's reading of what that clause asks of this client, the paragraph in the client's own procedure that answers it, and the compliance lead's reasons for calling that sufficient.

## 2. What changed since we last looked?

Each new version is rendered into a change map; an agent is dispatched to investigate the change.

Because references are anchored to source material, change arrives already joined to the record.

*Regulations and guidance · financial filings and debt agreements · contracts · your own procedures and policies.*

## 3. Why did we decide that?

**Every position your team ever took, still answerable years later: who held it, what it rested on, and who disagreed.**

An assessment replays exactly as it was recorded: the conclusion, the version of the passage it cited, who made the call, and when.

Exeges is the system of record for what your organization knows and how it came to know it.

It is the evidence layer where human and agent work compounds: every claim verified against source, every change investigated on arrival, every session building on the last.

## Today's systems lose twice

**What you learn evaporates.** An analyst spends a day pulling an answer out of a dozen sources. The answer ships; the work that produced it evaporates: what was read, what was ruled out, why the conclusion held. Six months later, a colleague re-derives it from zero. Agents amplify the loss: more claims, faster, with no memory of their own prior work and a willingness to be confidently wrong. A claim you can't trace was always a liability; now there are thousands more of them, arriving daily.

**What changes goes unnoticed.** In a large, living body of sources, the highest-signal event is change — and change is exactly what nobody reads for. When researchers compared years of corporate filings version against version, the quiet edits reliably predicted trouble ahead — and the market barely moved, because nobody reads the new version against the old ("Lazy Prices," Cohen, Malloy & Nguyen). The costliest questions are the ones nobody knew to ask.

The market has answered with two kinds of tools, and each preserves the wrong half. Research platforms merge a dozen sources into a good answer, but the answer is ephemeral: non-portable, gone when the tab closes. Agent-governance platforms log every step an agent takes — tool calls, approval gates, tamper-evident audit trails — but an execution log records what the agent *did*, not what is *true*.

Today's platforms were built for an era when discovery was the expensive part of knowledge work. Agents ended that era. Answers are cheap now; trust and durability are the new scarcity.

## What makes Exeges different

One mechanism closes both losses: a loop that runs continuously over your corpus — the full body of sources your organization works from.

**Your corpus is a living structure.** Exeges models what your sources do to each other — versions, references, amendments, dependencies — not just what they say. Every relationship is clickable through to the text that proves it. And the structure moves: new versions, new documents, new data, arriving all the time.

**Change is investigated on arrival.** A new version lands — a filing, a regulation, a contract. Every difference is discovered mechanically, section by section; nobody had to read for it. The sections you own fire, and a fired watch doesn't send you a notification — it dispatches an agent to investigate, before anyone has asked a question.

**Nothing enters the record unverified.** Agent findings are checked against source excerpts before they are accepted — an agent cannot file a claim the evidence doesn't support. Machine output stays provisional until it survives review, and what matters is adjudicated by people accountable for the result.

**Findings live on the corpus.** Accepted findings are filed as annotations: on the document itself, anchored to the passage that proves them. Not a report about your sources — durable marks on them, each one replayable, claim by claim, back to preserved evidence. Not logs of tool calls; the chain of proof.

**Every finding is a new event.** An annotation can alert the analyst who owns the section — or put another agent to work. The next session, human or agent, starts from everything already established, with coverage observed from system behavior, not asserted: what has been examined, what was skimmed, what remains untouched. The loop doesn't wait for the next question. Your corpus keeps working when nobody is asking.
